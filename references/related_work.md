# Related work and scope of the comparison

The result studied here is an accuracy-dependent sample law for **proper selection from a fixed finite hypothesis class**, with passive examples from a fixed, arbitrary conditional-state table on a known uniform support of size $`M`$. For fixed $`a,\epsilon,H,\delta`$, the strict interval $`\Gamma_k(a)\lt \epsilon\lt \Gamma_{k-1}(a)`$ determines the exponent $`M^{1-1/k}`$. The lower bound permits arbitrary collective quantum processing; a procedure that measures arrivals individually already achieves this support exponent for fixed $`H`$.

The moment duality, polynomial-copy correspondence, collision exponents, and distinction between passive data and repeated observations all have substantial precedents. The contribution claimed here is their precise connection to the **proper tilted-risk decision** and its accuracy thresholds in this source model. This is a focused comparison, not a claim that the underlying methods or the general need for repeated observations are new.

| Frongillo, Mehta, Morgan and Waggoner, [*Multi-Observation Regression*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/frongillo19a.html), AISTATS 2019, Secs. 2–5 and Theorems 1–3 | Learns higher-order conditional targets from ordinary examples by constructing multi-observation examples from nearby inputs. | Casalaina-Martin, Frongillo, Morgan and Waggoner, [*Multi-Observation Elicitation*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v65/casalaina-martin17a.html), COLT 2017, Definitions 2 and 5, Theorem 7, Corollaries 10 and 13 | Formalizes losses receiving several independent outcomes from the same distribution and proves tradeoffs between observation count and report dimension. | Adibi and Krysta, [*Optimal Rényi and Logarithmic Additive Functional Entropy Estimation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28999), arXiv:2610.28999v1 (2026), Appendix I, Theorem I.1 and Corollary I.2 | Gives exact weighted relative-approximation duality: an annihilating signed measure yields matching-moment probability laws with expectation ratio $`(1+d)/(1-d)`$. | This is the scalar duality behind the two equivalent definitions of $`\Gamma_r(a)`$; it is explicitly inherited. The additional steps here are the two-action quantum-state embedding, concentration for a frozen finite table, and matching passive-context extraction. |

Adibi–Krysta's main Theorem 2.2 concerns a fixed scalar function with a specified leading power at zero. It should not simply be substituted for the present theorem: writing the classical diagonal witness as $`q_{x,1}=p_x/M`$ makes its target involve $`e^{aM q_{x,1}}`$, with a known uniform context marginal and an $`M`$-dependent rescaling. This observation identifies a difference in the stated models; it is not a claim that classical functional-estimation methods cannot prove the present law.

Other methodological ancestors are important. Tian, Kong and Valiant's [*Learning Populations of Parameters*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.02707) (NeurIPS 2017) develops moment-based inference for populations of coins observed several times. Acharya, Orlitsky, Suresh and Tyagi's [*The Complexity of Estimating Rényi Entropy*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.1000) gives the familiar integer-order collision exponent. Brun's [*Measuring Polynomial Functions of States*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0401067) establishes the use of several copies to measure polynomial state functionals. None of these ingredients is presented here as a new quantum effect.

Similarly, Poisson repetition gives the standard identity $`\mathbb E[p^K]=e^{a(p-1)}`$ for $`K\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(a)`$. Bernoulli-factory constructions and their use in exponential transformations predate this account; see Dughmi, Hartline, Kleinberg and Niazadeh, [*Bernoulli Factories and Black-Box Reductions in Mechanism Design*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04143). A random repetition count with finite expectation must be distinguished from a deterministic maximum number of copies per context.

## Public related source-model analyses

The public [Quantum-Example-Entropy frozen-source analysis](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Quantum-Example-Entropy/blob/d1077fac179d33972a63f57d60d54b822721b328/science/frozen_sources.md), Theorem S1, supplies a related frozen-table mechanism. Its coherent-example simulation error includes the address-collision term $`\binom{T}{2}\sum_x p_x^2`$, alongside a label-collision term. The present result uses higher occupancy levels and moment twins to determine a proper nonlinear-risk decision threshold. The two tasks and access models differ; the frozen-source and collision reasoning are material prior context.

The public [Quantum-Trajectory-Cloning-Cost analysis](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Quantum-Trajectory-Cloning-Cost/blob/94798b20a91637dbbed6ddfb0b9d0dd92560040e/science/analysis.md) proves a repeat-label protocol with $`M+1`$ expected source copies that produces two conditional states while preserving the original label law. That full-output requirement differs from scalar risk selection and from uniformly selecting a repeated context. Its proposed matching lower bound and known-assignment separation are explicitly marked unverified there and are not used as established results here.

## Scope and limitation

The strongest limitation is the classical core of the lower-bound example. Commuting two-qubit states already force the staircase, and separate measurements attain its support exponent for fixed $`H`$. The result therefore concerns the information supplied by the source and the precision of the learning objective. It establishes no quantum processing advantage, no new general collision exponent, and no unrestricted learnability obstruction for all nonlinear quantum losses.

The comparison is restricted to the sources and theorem locations identified above. [BibTeX records](bibliography.bib) give structured citations for the principal external sources. Critical accuracies $`\epsilon=\Gamma_r(a)`$, nonuniform context laws, smooth conditional-state families, and joint limits in class size, tilt, precision or degree require additional analysis.
